Principles

  • No missing observation is imputed or interpolated by this dashboard. A value missing at the source is missing on the dashboard, and the reason is recorded. Survey estimates (such as NCES's) are shown as their publishers report them, and the one modeled figure, the composition illustration, is labeled as such.
  • Sources are preserved. Where available, archived copies (Internet Archive Wayback Machine) are linked beside cited sources. Sources without a public archive copy are preserved privately so figures can be re-checked; DOI citations keep their persistent links. Original source links and their archived copies are checked monthly.
  • Published and computed values are labeled. When an agency publishes a rate, that rate is shown. When it publishes only the components, the dashboard divides them and labels the result "computed". Third-party calculations are labeled as such.
  • Every series records its definition. What counts as a vacancy, which positions are covered, when it is measured, and what the denominator is. These differ across sources, and the dashboard shows them side by side.
  • Verification is recorded point by point. Each state's entry says which figures were checked against the agency's own publication (not secondary reports), which could only be confirmed indirectly, and which were carried over from the October 2026 baseline workbook without re-checking.

National series (BLS)

All from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Every series is retrieved directly from the BLS Public Data API v2. (The series keys below, such as JTSJOR and PAYEMS, are the familiar FRED names, kept so the downloadable files stay stable; the values are BLS's own.) Seasonally adjusted unless noted.

Series retrieved automatically

Request: POST https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/ with series IDs of the form JTS + 6-digit industry code + 000000000 + element (JOL level, JOR openings rate, HIR hires, QUR quits, LDR layoffs & discharges). The industry table uses the latest month (no later than the headline month) in which every industry shown has every measure: openings level and the openings, hires, quits, and layoffs rates. If no such month is within two months of the headline month, the update stops and the site keeps the last good data. Industries follow BLS's JOLTS hierarchy.

NCES School Pulse Panel

Publisher. National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), Institute of Education Sciences; data collected by the U.S. Census Bureau.

File. StaffVac.xlsx ("Staff Vacancies" workbook, linked from nces.ed.gov/surveys/spp/results.asp). Rows used: sheet SVaverage, "All public schools", variables PCTVAC1_*_oct (teaching roles), PCTVAC2_*_oct (non-teaching roles), PCTVAC1TOT_oct and PCTVAC2TOT_oct (totals), each with its standard error; sheet All Schools, variable StaffVac1_TOT_CAT1 (share of schools with one, or more than one, teaching vacancy).

Question. From the October 2024 questionnaire: for each role, principals entered "Total number of positions at my school (filled + vacant)" and "Number of vacancies", counting each part-time position as 1. NCES reports vacancies as a percent of positions.

Transformations. None to the values. October waves are mapped to school years (October 2024 → 2024-25). 95% confidence intervals in tooltips are ±1.96 × the published standard error.

Caveats. NCES calls SPP results "experimental data." Estimates are rounded to whole percents. The sample is about 4,000 public schools. The 2024-25 survey cycle ended in July 2025, after NCES staff cuts, and no further staffing wave has been announced.

    October benchmarks on state panelsNot-seasonally-adjusted JOLTS openings rates for October (series JTU923000000000000JOR and JTU000000000000000JOR), mapped to the school year starting that fall.
    Computed state ratesUnfilled ÷ (filled + unfilled), or unfilled ÷ total positions, from the components the agency published. Each computed point is labeled.
    Illinois rates by position categoryUnfilled ÷ (filled + unfilled) from ISBE/IWERC Table 5 (SY23–SY25) and the archived SY22 data set. "All staff" sums the four categories.
    Illinois staffing growth and unfilled ratesChange in filled FTE by category = latest filled count ÷ earliest filled count − 1. Filled counts come from ISBE's staff records (EIS), as published with the unfilled data (IWERC/ISBE Table 5); ISBE pairs each year's unfilled FTE with the prior year's filled FTE, so the filled counts describe 2021-22 (SY22 and SY23 rows) through 2023-24 (SY25 row). The mapping is recorded in data/states.json (filled_school_year). Unfilled rates are the latest year's, by ISBE's definition. Enrollment change uses statewide enrollment from the Illinois Report Card public data sets (statewide row, "# Student Enrollment"; 1,869,325 in 2021-22 and 1,851,290 in 2023-24), recorded in data/states.json (illinois_enrollment). The paraprofessional trend in the text compares that category's unfilled rate in its peak year with the latest year.
    Composition illustrationAn illustration, not a count. State & local education openings (JOLTS) are divided pro-rata: the local-government share of state & local education payroll jobs (CES9093161101 ÷ [CES9093161101 + CES9092161101]) stands in for K-12, and the teacher share of public K-12 staff FTE (3,228,895 ÷ 6,795,470, NCES Digest Table 213.20, fall 2022) splits K-12 into teachers and other staff. Shown as shares only, under the assumption that every group has the same vacancy rate.
    Rates relative to their own 2015–19 averageEach month's seasonally adjusted rate ÷ that series' mean monthly rate from January 2015 through December 2019, × 100, for the measure selected (openings, hires, quits, or layoffs). The 3-month average option is applied after indexing.
    Public school staff and students since 2000From NCES Digest Table 213.10: full-time-equivalent teachers, instructional aides, and all staff, each ÷ its fall 2000 value × 100. Enrollment is NCES's teachers × pupils per teacher for the same fall, which reproduces NCES's published public enrollment (e.g., 50,796,445 in fall 2019). Students per teacher and per staff member are NCES's published ratios. Fall 2001 is not in the table and is left blank.

    Known issues and open questions

    • Iowa's earlier years. Iowa documents its 12-month posting window (August 20 to August 20) only for its latest release. The dashboard assumes the earlier releases used the same window when placing them on the school-year axis. Iowa's own dashboard labels the figures the same way.
    • Virginia 2022-23 is a third-party calculation (SwailLandis, March 2023) from VDOE's Build-a-Table data, with a denominator labeled "FTE Teachers". The same brief also cites 2.6%. VDOE's own published series starts in 2023-24 (3.9%). VDOE's site blocks automated access, so the Board presentation's 2024-25 figure (3.4%) and rate definition were confirmed through a summary of that document.
    • Illinois SY22 uses a same-year filled count; ISBE's convention from SY23 on uses the prior year's. The SY22 point is not strictly comparable.
    • Colorado 2024-25 has no rate: CDE's workbook for that year has no total-positions field.
    • Nebraska response among nonpublic schools was 57.8% in 2025-26; totals combine public and nonpublic schools. A different total (107 vacant, public districts only) appears in the Learning Policy Institute's scan.
    • South Carolina reporting coverage varies from 71 to 83 entities across years.
    • JOLTS figures are revised: the latest month is preliminary, and annual benchmark revisions can change the whole history.
    • School Pulse Panel has no data after October 2024 and may not resume.
