Principles

  • Nothing is estimated or interpolated. A value missing at the source is missing on the dashboard, and the reason is recorded.
  • Published and computed values are labeled. When an agency publishes a rate, that rate is shown. When it publishes only the components, the dashboard divides them and labels the result "computed". Third-party calculations are labeled as such.
  • Every series records its definition. What counts as a vacancy, which positions are covered, when it is measured, and what the denominator is. These differ across sources, and the dashboard shows them side by side.
  • Verification is recorded point by point. Each state's entry says which figures were checked against the agency's own publication (not secondary reports), which could only be confirmed indirectly, and which were carried over from the October 2026 baseline workbook without re-checking.

National series (BLS)

All from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Every series is retrieved directly from the BLS Public Data API v2. (The series keys below, such as JTSJOR and PAYEMS, are the familiar FRED names, kept so the downloadable files stay stable; the values are BLS's own.) Seasonally adjusted unless noted.

Series retrieved automatically

Request: POST https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/ with series IDs of the form JTS + 6-digit industry code + 000000000 + element (JOL level, JOR openings rate, HIR hires, QUR quits, LDR layoffs & discharges). The industry table uses the latest month for 21 industries; mining & logging and transportation, warehousing & utilities are not returned by the API and are omitted.

NCES School Pulse Panel

Publisher. National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), Institute of Education Sciences; data collected by the U.S. Census Bureau.

File. StaffVac.xlsx ("Staff Vacancies" workbook, linked from nces.ed.gov/surveys/spp/results.asp). Rows used: sheet SVaverage, "All public schools", variables PCTVAC1_*_oct (teaching roles), PCTVAC2_*_oct (non-teaching roles), PCTVAC1TOT_oct and PCTVAC2TOT_oct (totals), each with its standard error; sheet All Schools, variable StaffVac1_TOT_CAT1 (share of schools with one, or more than one, teaching vacancy).

Question. From the October 2024 questionnaire: for each role, principals entered "Total number of positions at my school (filled + vacant)" and "Number of vacancies", counting each part-time position as 1. NCES reports vacancies as a percent of positions.

Transformations. None to the values. October waves are mapped to school years (October 2024 → 2024-25). 95% confidence intervals in tooltips are ±1.96 × the published standard error.

Caveats. NCES calls SPP results "experimental data." Estimates are rounded to whole percents. The sample is about 4,000 public schools. The 2024-25 survey cycle ended in July 2025, after NCES staff cuts, and no further staffing wave has been announced.

State & local education openings (JOLTS) × the local-government share of state & local education employment (CES9093161101 ÷ [CES9093161101 + CES9092161101]) gives "K-12"; that × the teacher share of public K-12 staff FTE gives "K-12 teachers". Teacher share = 3,228,895 ÷ 6,795,470 FTE (47.5%), the United States row of Digest of Education Statistics Table 213.20 (staff by type of assignment, fall 2022; NCES Common Core of Data, State Nonfiscal Survey 2022-23). Verified against the table on 2026-10-10: the row's components sum exactly to the total. Teachers include imputed prekindergarten teachers where states did not report them, and Utah's staff counts are imputed. It is the most recent year the Digest has published. Assumes every group has the same vacancy rate.

    Known issues and open questions

    • Iowa's earlier years. Iowa documents its 12-month posting window (August 20 to August 20) only for its latest release. The dashboard assumes the earlier releases used the same window when placing them on the school-year axis. Iowa's own dashboard labels the figures the same way.
    • Virginia 2022-23 is a third-party calculation (SwailLandis, March 2023) from VDOE's Build-a-Table data, with a denominator labeled "FTE Teachers". The same brief also cites 2.6%. VDOE's own published series starts in 2023-24 (3.9%). VDOE's site blocks automated access, so the Board presentation's 2024-25 figure (3.4%) and rate definition were confirmed through a summary of that document.
    • Illinois SY22 uses a same-year filled count; ISBE's convention from SY23 on uses the prior year's. The SY22 point is not strictly comparable.
    • Colorado 2024-25 has no rate: CDE's workbook for that year has no total-positions field.
    • Nebraska response among nonpublic schools was 57.8% in 2025-26; totals combine public and nonpublic schools. A different total (107 vacant, public districts only) appears in the Learning Policy Institute's scan.
    • South Carolina reporting coverage varies from 71 to 83 entities across years.
    • JOLTS figures are revised: the latest month is preliminary, and annual benchmark revisions can change the whole history.
    • School Pulse Panel has no data after October 2024 and may not resume.
